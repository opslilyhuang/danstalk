# Comedy Writing Process

## Pre-Interview Technique for Comedy Writing

**When to use:** When conducting pre-interview for comedy shows to gather material for scripts.

### Fundamental Difference from Regular Interviews
Stand-up pre-interview is **creative pre-interview**:
- You have a purpose: to form a work
- The work is co-created by you and the guest
- Unlike regular interviews that just record what the interviewee says

### The "Don't Ask Why" Principle
**Do not ask "why"** - this is an interview technique.

**Why:** Most people haven't sorted out why they do things. If you ask, you might get:
- A wrong answer
- Getting stuck
- A lie

**Better approach:** Break it down into individual questions, deduce the logic yourself, then connect them together.

### Capturing Voice and Style
- Master the guest's tone and speaking rhythm
- Similar to writing novel characters
- If the style is weird enough, it's easier to write
- If a person has nothing distinctive, it's very hard to write

### The "Classroom" Technique for Generating Jokes
Thinking of jokes during pre-interview is **mandatory** for writers.

**How to do it:**
1. Imagine it's a classroom
2. The interviewee is the teacher
3. Interject/follow up on their words (接下茬)
4. Keep following up and it might become a joke
5. If worried about offending: whisper to someone nearby or write it down
6. **Key:** Cannot stagnate, cannot just listen

### Loving the Character/Guest
- Love the guest like loving a character
- If communication is difficult: treat them as an NPC, isolate the emotion
- Think: "If this guest could say it this way, that would be great, so cute"
- This is standing on their side
- Isolate difficult emotions and return to the script

## Small Context vs Big Context Structure

**When to use:** When planning a comedy set of 15-20+ minutes.

### Understand Small Context (小情境)
**Definition:** Individual jokes or story segments within your set.

**Example structure for a 20-minute set:**
- Segment 1: "I was beaten by my dad when young"
- Segment 2: "I became a father, how I face my child"
- Segment 3: "I imagine my child growing up, will he have children"

**Alternative:** Small contexts can be completely unrelated (being beaten by father, working as delivery person, something about girlfriend).

### Understand Big Context (大情境)
**Definition:** How you connect all small contexts together.

**Purpose:** Show the completeness of your personality and your growth. You must show growth on stage. The big context creates a unified experience from separate pieces.

### The "Truth Revealing Moment" (揭示宇宙真相的时刻)
This occurs when small and big contexts perfectly merge.

**Example:** Anthony Jeselnik's performance:
- Starts very dark
- Becomes pitiful and wronged
- Shows betrayal, exploitation, threats to family
- Concludes: "I'm still this kind of person, I've grown again"
- Effect: Reveals truth - he is this way, but won't change

**Note:** This level requires significant length (more than 15 minutes). Even the greatest comedians achieve this only a few times in their career.

### Implementation Approach
- Don't obsessively pursue the "truth revealing moment"
- Focus on connecting your small contexts with logical threads
- Let growth and completeness emerge naturally

## Script Reading Meeting Process (读稿会)

**When to use:** When comedy scripts need review and improvement.

### Definition and Basic Form
- Script reading meetings can be big or small
- Basic form: several people with creative ability gather together, take turns reading their scripts
- People in the room judge each other's scripts
- **Critical distinction:** Judgment is through modification, not negation - only to make it better

### Scale Variations
- **Small scale:** Three beginners, after weekly open mic, read each other's scripts together
- **Large scale:** TV shows like Roast Convention - writers write guest scripts, gather to read together, decide script usage iteratively

### The Consensus Principle (NOT Democratic Voting)
**Creation requires absolute standards, not voting.**

- If done by democratic voting, it can't work
- Three proposals won't move forward if everything is voted on
- But it's not truly dictatorial either
- A consensus magically forms in the room
- Everyone has a fuzzy standard in their heart
- Everyone must arrive at the consensus together

### Role of Director in Script Reading
- Script reading always needs the head director present to correct deviations
- During script reading, it's easy to self-indulge
- This is why the script reading system exists

### Director vs. Writer Relationship
- Split into two positions for a reason
- Need two voices to counterbalance
- They need to cooperate and struggle
- If writers do everything: content becomes too skewed and self-indulgent
- If directors do everything: content may become tacky

### Future Evolution
For stand-up shows, writers and directors will eventually become one. The next head director will likely emerge from writers or actors.
