# Performance Preparation and Mindset

## Goal Setting

**Correct Goal:** Become a "stronger comedian" (Long-term career perspective)

**Incorrect Goal:** Make this specific show "explode" (Short-term)

**View career as:**
- A long poker game, not a single hand
- Meaningful failure is far more valuable than meaningless success

## Preparation Rituals

**Establish baseline preparation:**
- Mirror practice
- Recording review
- Mental rehearsal
- Even if it feels useless, do it - establishes baseline

## Writing Phase Mindset

**Accept imperfection:**
- Accept you will never be fully "ready"
- Purpose of preparation: "make it" (finish the work), not reach perfect standard
- If you reach internal standard, you feel empty and creation stops

**Writing goals:**
- DO NOT think: "I must write a joke that kills you" (aim for 'bombing')
- Think: "I want to write a joke better than before" or "I want to improve the previous one"
- Attitude: "I don't know if it works, but I'll try"

**Performance phase goals:**
- Once in theater, you CAN aim to 'bomb' (get big reaction)
- But this mindset should NOT exist during writing

## On-Stage Crisis Protocol

**IF jokes don't land (不响):**

**DO:**
- Stick to the Verbatim Script
- Finish confidently

**DO NOT:**
- Panic
- Use fillers ('uh/ah')
- Randomly interact with audience
- Insert old jokes not in the script

## Post-Show Analysis

**The Two Types of Failure:**

**Meaningful Failure:**
- Failed while sticking to the script
- Allows adjustment of specific bits/material
- Provides learning data

**Meaningless Failure:**
- Failed by panicking, deviating from script, or ad-libbing poorly
- Provides no learning data
- Wasted opportunity

**Attribution of Error:**

**Ignore (External factors):**
- Audience was cold
- Mic issues
- Lighting problems
- Previous act bombed
- These explain "why this show failed"

**Focus (Internal growth factors):**
- Your preparation
- Your execution
- Your ability to handle pressure
- These explain "why you aren't becoming a stronger comedian"
