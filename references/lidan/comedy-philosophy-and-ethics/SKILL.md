# Comedy Philosophy and Ethics

## Values, Exposure, and Influence

### Nature of Values
- Values are expressed through any life description (e.g., drink choice)
- Stage persona reflects true personality ("Card playing shows character")

### Consistency Rule
- Values in jokes can contradict if there is a reasonable explanation
- If not explained, the audience gets confused ("brain stalls")

### Impact and Influence
- Comedy creates an "illusion of change" (enlightenment/confusion)
- **Warning:** Do not use comedy to incite/influence (demagoguery); it is dangerous
- Be a comedian, not a speaker

### Exposure and Sincerity
- Exposure must be measured, not total (total exposure is uncomfortable)
- Humor techniques allow uncomfortable truths to be spoken and accepted

### Personality and Suitability
- Personality is cultivated (civilization), not just genetic
- Believe you can improve
- **Requirement:** Kind-hearted people are suited for comedy; dark-hearted people cannot do it (their darkness is real, not funny)

## Audience Relationship Philosophy

### Core Principle - Equal Footing (平视)
The relationship between comedian and audience must be equal.

- The process of finding your own audience is moving
- When you find your audience: You feel they understand you, no explanation needed
- Even partial understanding is enough

### Anti-Pandering Principle
- This moving relationship cannot be begged for
- When you pander to the audience on stage, they know it
- Consequence: Your relationship won't be equal, won't be at eye level
- Pandering may work temporarily but is not sustainable

### Being a "Good Person" Requirement
This is not just moral advice but rational analysis.

- If you're really not a good person, you can't stand on stage continuously and have people love you
- Reasoning: You need to make people laugh, and laughter is a positive emotion
- If someone is willing to be a stand-up comedian, they can't be that bad

### Performer's Mindset
- "I still just tell my stuff"
- "I know it will be funny, this is part of my plan"
- Accept variations: Sometimes laughter is loud, sometimes soft
- There's rarely a complete miss
- When audience laughs as planned, the relationship feels tight

### Dealing with Audience Variation
- Focus on improvement: "What problems existed today, can it be better?"
- Don't obsess over individual show variations

### Impact on Audience
- Comedy will definitely influence people
- This influence is subtle and gradual
- The younger the audience, the greater the impact
- **Responsibility:** You should be a good person even more
