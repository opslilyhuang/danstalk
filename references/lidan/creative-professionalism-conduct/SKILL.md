# Creative Professionalism and Conduct

## Core Professional Mindset

Reframe creative work as a profession, not just an art form or hobby.

**Apply the "Be fucking professional" standard:**
- Handle unglamorous tasks with rigor (e.g., negotiating at 2 AM to fix script errors)
- Maintain quality despite fatigue
- Treat professional standards as evolving guidance, not absolute dogma

**Crystallize vague knowledge:**
- Stop asking repetitive "old questions" (e.g., "Why is the audience cold?")
- Seek and record fixed answers to basic problems
- Stop treating fundamental perspectives as vague

## Professional Conduct Rules

**Prohibit these behaviors regardless of work quality:**
- Lateness
- Scolding clients
- Berating colleagues for "not understanding creation"
- Acting entitled or displaying "artist temperament"

**Rationale:**
- Even excellent work ("特别特别特别特别好") does not justify entitlement
- Entitled behavior harms creation and professional relationships
- Success is measured by work quality, not by artistic pretension

## The Battlefield Principle

**Stay engaged:**
- Do NOT leave the "battlefield" (work environment), "comrades," or "enemies"
- Leaving the work rhythm is more fatal to creativity than leaving life/learning

**Learn by doing:**
- Apply "replacement of training with competition" (以赛代练)
- Learning happens through practice and making mistakes
- No practice equals no learning

## Practical Utility Focus

**Prioritize actionable advice:**
- Focus on "insider" experience over abstract theory
- Treat this guidance as a practical tool for immediate application
- Assume basic technical knowledge (rhythm, structure) is already known
- "In the art industry, only laws that are useful to you are laws."

**Iterate on knowledge:**
- If guidance is unsatisfactory, actively challenge and update it
- Share new insights based on personal understanding
